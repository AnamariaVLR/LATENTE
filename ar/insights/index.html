Between 1,850 and 2,100 metres in southern Huila and Nariño, the coffee tree does something it cannot do lower down: it takes its time. Cooler nights slow the cherry's maturation from roughly 180 days after flowering to 210–240, and that extra month is where the complexity of a High Bloom is built — sugars accumulate, acids soften into structure, and the seed densifies.

We do not pick by colour. A refractometer at the tree gives the cherry's soluble sugar in degrees Brix, and our harvest window opens at 20 °Bx and closes at 24. Below it, a coffee is thin and grassy; above it, it drifts toward over-ripe ferment. Pickers pass the same tree three to five times over a season, taking only what the instrument confirms.

Every lot is then float-sorted in water and inspected by hand, with a working threshold of at least ninety-five percent fully ripe cherry before it is allowed to enter fermentation. The cupping table is where these decisions are audited: our House Coffees are curated from lots scoring 86 and above on the SCA protocol.

التجفيف · ملاحظة 01 · أغسطس 2026

أبطأ خطوة هي الأكثر حسماً

قد تُقطف القهوة وتُخمَّر بإتقان ثم تضيع على سرير التجفيف. نُبقي الحبوب الخضراء ضمن نافذة ضيقة من الرطوبة والنشاط المائي، تُبلَغ ببطء.

النشاط المائي
≤ 0.60 aw — قبل التخزين
مدة التجفيف
12–18 days — أسرّة مرتفعة، ظل
حرارة الحبة
≤ 40 °C — لا تُتجاوز أبداً
سماكة الطبقة
≤ 3 cm — تُقلَّب 6 مرات يومياً

Drying decides whether everything achieved at altitude survives the journey. Dried too fast, the bean's outer layers harden around a still-moist core; the coffee cups well for a month and then fades. Dried too slowly, moulds and unwanted fermentation take hold. The target is not a number but a curve: a steady descent to 10.5–11.5% moisture over roughly two weeks, with bean temperature never allowed above 40 °C.

We dry on raised beds under shade cloth, in layers no deeper than three centimetres, turned six times a day so no bean sits in its own humidity. Moisture is read daily with a calibrated meter; water activity — the measure that governs shelf stability — is confirmed at 0.60 aw or below before a lot is rested. Only then does a coffee earn its place in the House.

This is why we speak of precision rather than craft alone. Craft is the hand that turns the bed; precision is the instrument that tells the hand when to stop.
